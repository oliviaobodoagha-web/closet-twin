The problem: getting dressed, and buying shoes that fit

12–18 minevery morning choosing an outfit (UK surveys, ~2,000 women each); 105 min for me on a real recruiting day
24–31%of online apparel orders are returned (Coresight 2023, ECDB 2026)
53–70%of those returns are about size and fit (Coresight, McKinsey)

Me: a recruiting day holds a campus event, career office hours and consulting coffee chats, each with its own dress code, plus a cold walk. And I wear a women's 12 wide. "Available in 12" doesn't say "wide, polished, walkable all day"; that lives only in reviews. My last search took about a week.

What exists: Alta does closet + daily outfits + shopping well. Reviewers of Alta, Daydream, Doppl, Indyx and ChatGPT-as-stylist keep saying the same things: generic or random looks, ignores context and past dislikes, wrong links, no fit check.

What's new: a stylist with a point of view, and rules in code

Sample profile: her read of the inspiration board, archetypes shown as pictures, and the goal look + strategy she writes.

  • One stylist, Margaux-Sloan: archetype blend, color season, inspiration board, check-ins and house rules → a read of me (signature, goal picture, strategy) that every styling agent receives
  • "No" is enforced in code: a Not for me, a Tell-box tag or a house rule becomes a taste ban ("men's-style loafers", while slim feminine loafers pass), checked on plans, alternates, picks, shop results and picture prompts
  • Stylist rules in code: no two statement colors, no brown shoes under black-and-white, nothing casual on a recruiting day, no all-neutral day, no repeats in 14 days, nothing not in the closet
  • Fingerprints: same inputs → the same saved pick, instantly, $0
  • Real links only: a shop link is kept only if the web search really returned or opened that page
  • Pictures on tap, never by surprise; a repeat picture is reused for $0

Shopping in my size, with links that are real

  • Find what's missing: 3 pieces + an outfit each for $0.0015; Draw them = 6 mock-ups (~$0.18) only on a tap
  • Live shop: shopper-style queries, size evidence ("12 W listed"), then Fit Tailor → Critic; a Critic revise triggers a second search that hears the Critic
  • Bug I found: the app trusted only links the model cited, and it rarely cites in JSON, so 4 of 11 live shops lost real finds. Now every URL the search returned or opened is trusted and listed; unseen links are still dropped
  • Honest cards: an unconfirmed size gets a grey note, never a green ✓; an unseen price says "Price at store"
  • A request that breaks a no ("pleated mini skirt" vs "never very short") is answered before any paid search, $0

Kept safe, and tested

  • Prompt injection: reviews, page titles and calendar fields are cleaned; instruction-like text is quarantined; the rest is fenced as untrusted data
  • Privacy: the agent that reads the web never holds measurements; the one that holds them never reads the web. No photo leaves without consent; GPS stripped
  • Malfunction: invented clothes or links force a revise and never win best round; bans and house rules checked in code
  • Photos of a person: try-on keeps exact face and body, no slimming; graders use a fictional test-kit person
  • Overspending: per-run and per-day budgets checked before every call, 14-call loop guard, ≤ 2 Critic revisions

Limits, and what's next

  • Live shop $0.055–0.12; listings rarely confirm width or comfort
  • Bans are word-based: a black "Cloud Loafer" slipped past "black leather loafers"; "loafers with a wide toe box" was refused
  • Try-on likeness: recent try-ons don't look enough like me
