The problem: shopping for clothes, then deciding what to wear

~35 mina day for me, between shopping for clothes and choosing what to wear
24–31%of clothes bought online are sent back (Coresight 2023, ECDB 2026)
53–70%of those returns happen because the size or fit is wrong (Coresight, McKinsey)

My day: in recruiting season one day can hold a campus event, career office hours and coffee chats with consultants, each with its own dress code, plus a cold walk across campus.

My feet: I wear a women's 12 wide. A store saying "available in 12" doesn't tell me it's wide, polished and comfortable enough to walk in all day. My last search for shoes like that took about a week.

What's out there: apps like Alta, Daydream and ChatGPT can suggest outfits, but people say the looks feel random, ignore what they already said they dislike, and link to the wrong products.

What's different: a stylist who actually listens

She explains styles with pictures, then sketches the look she's dressing you toward and how she'll get you there.

  • She gets to know you: your style, your best colors and an inspiration board you share, so every outfit has a point of view
  • "No" means no: if I say black loafers look too masculine on me, she never suggests them again, in outfits, shopping or pictures. The app checks every suggestion against my no's before I see it, so it doesn't depend on the AI remembering
  • She follows real styling rules: no sneakers at a recruiting event, no brown shoes with a black-and-white outfit, no wearing the same look twice in two weeks, and only clothes I actually own
  • Same day, same answer: if nothing changed, she shows the outfit she already picked, instantly and for free
  • Only real shopping links, and pictures are made only when I ask for them

Learning my style

Color Analyst finds my best colors from a face photo

Body Estimator estimates my sizes from a full-length photo

Style Analyst matches me to style types (classic, romantic…)

Stylist Read writes Margaux-Sloan's summary of me: signature, goal look, plan

They never talk to me directly: I only hear Margaux-Sloan. Regular code, not another AI, decides who works when and double-checks their answers.

Safety protocol

  • Hidden instructions: a review or web page that says "ignore your rules" is cleaned out and treated as plain text, never as an order
  • Privacy: the helper that searches the web never sees my measurements; photos are only sent with my OK, and their location data is removed
  • No making things up: an outfit with a piece I don't own, or a link nobody visited, is rejected
  • Respecting the real person: try-ons keep my face and body as they are, with no slimming or retouching
  • Spending limits: every run and every day has a budget, and a run is stopped if it keeps going in circles

What I learned, and what's next

  • Reviews for fit: my original goal was to use reviews to decide fit and size. Requiring that evidence left too few products to choose from. A different prompt could use review data without filtering out so much
  • One tester so far: the results are from me, not yet from classmates
  • Speed: a new plan takes 20 seconds to 3½ minutes
  • Store searches cost 5–12 cents, and stores rarely list width or comfort
  • No's are matched on words: a "Cloud Loafer" slipped past "black leather loafers", and "loafers with a wide toe box" was blocked
  1. A fitting-room avatar of me and try-on pictures that look truer to the outfit
  2. Retailer product data with real size and width info: cheaper than live search, {{FEED_COST}} per person per month
  3. A guided walkthrough for first-time users
  4. Smarter no's that understand style, toe shape and material, and ask "is this what you meant?"
  5. A pilot with 10 classmates to measure time saved and returns avoided
